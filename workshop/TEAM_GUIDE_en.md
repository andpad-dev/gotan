# How Teams Work

Each team works on **one scenario as a whole team**, dividing its **questions among pairs**. With four people, that means two pairs of two.

In a time-limited workshop, prioritize leaving a reproducible research trail over finishing every question.

## The 5 Minutes Before Choosing a Topic

1. Spend the first three minutes introducing yourselves, one person at a time: your name, Go experience, and what interests you today.
2. Use the remaining time to choose one topic from the [scenario list](SCENARIOS_en.md) and announce it in your team's Issue.

## The First 3 Minutes of the Exploration Workshop

Decide on your entry point and division of work.

1. As a team, open the scenario's **investigation entry points** or the category README, and decide which primary source to start with.
2. Split into pairs of two and divide the questions. If there are two questions, each pair takes one. If there are three or more, divide them into the first and second groups.
3. Finally, choose one person to assemble the team's findings. The coordinator also participates in the research.

When the number of questions does not divide evenly between the pairs, it is more productive for **both pairs to approach one question from different entry points** than for one pair to take an extra question.
For example, one pair can run the code in the Playground while the other reads the specification.

## How to Use the 45 Minutes

| Elapsed time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0-3 minutes | Decide on an entry point as a team and divide the questions between pairs |
| 3-25 minutes | Research in pairs and record findings in the Issue as you go |
| 25 minutes (20 minutes remaining) | Signal that it is okay to open the answer; open it now if you still have no clues |
| 25-35 minutes | Share discoveries between pairs and combine them into one comment |
| 35-45 minutes (10 minutes remaining) | Have the coordinator lead the team in writing the presentation summary |

If only one person in a pair is continuing to research, assign the other person a different entry point so you can split the work.

## When You Get Stuck

First return to the **investigation entry points** before the question. There should still be sources you have not opened. They are alternatives, not a sequence, so re-enter through whichever one looks most useful.

## Hints and Answers

- **You may open the hints from the beginning.** Use them as search-term suggestions and entry points to primary sources.
- **You may open the answers too.** The facilitator will give a signal when 20 minutes remain. Do not copy the conclusion; follow the research route written there and verify it yourself.
- When working through a scenario on your own, keep the hints closed and try for five minutes or make two different research attempts before opening them. That lets you enjoy the exploration itself.

**You do not need to finish every question.** Thoroughly investigating one question is more valuable for today's purpose.

## Comments to Leave in the Issue

Each time you research something, leave one comment containing the following. Do not leave a comment that only contains URLs.

```text
- Question investigated:
- Starting point:
- Operation/search terms:
- Location checked: <URL and section name>
- What we learned:
- Next question / not yet verified:
```

In Go Playground, press **Share** to create a shareable URL, then include the code, the displayed Go version, and the actual output in the same comment.

Near the end, the coordinator rearranges everyone's comments and the team confirms the following together.

```text
## Presentation summary
- Topic investigated:
- Initial observation:
- Research route: <starting point -> search terms/links -> primary source -> experiment>
- What we learned:
- What remains unknown:
- Most important discovery:
```

The coordinator should not create the conclusion alone. At the end, have each pair confirm that its evidence and summary are accurate.
